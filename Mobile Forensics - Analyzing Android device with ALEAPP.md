# Endpoint Analysis - Utilizing ALEAPP to analyze Android device artifacts

## Objective


The Android EDR Lab project aimed to analyze a crucial piece of evidence —an Android phone belonging to a victim in a murder investigation, involving financial debts, suspicious communication patterns, and last-known movements. The primary focus was to reconstruct the victim’s activities leading up to the incident by carefully analyzing artifacts extracted from the device including financial interactions and suspicious messages to GPS locations, chat conversation, their social interactions, and potential motivations behind the events. And this was done by the use of tools like ALEAPP, a powerful forensic utility tailored for Android artifacts, alongside other analysis techniques to extract and interpret key data from the victim's phone.

## Skills Learned

- Advanced understanding and practical application of Mobile Forensic in solving real-world cases.
- Proficiency in investigating GPS data, Financial transaction, Chat logs, and SMS messages.
- Utilization of Foresic tool tailored for parsing Android file systems and analyzing Android artifacts.
- Development of critical thinking and problem-solving skills in cybersecurity.

- Kali Linux

## Steps
1) First, based on the accounts of the witnesses and individuals close to the victim, it has become clear that the victim was interested in trading. This has led him to invest all of his money and acquire debt. So the first action is to identify the blockchain security of the trading application the victim used on his phone i.e the SHA256 of the app. By directing ALEAPP to the extracted file system of the victim's phone, I configured it to extract and process data from all available modules, including accounts, notifications, app interactions, and installed applications.

The output generated by ALEAPP provides a detailed and categorized report that facilitates the forensic analysis.

Through ALEAPP's output, I examined the details of installed applications on the victim’s phone. Installed apps on Android devices are not only identified by their display names but also by unique bundle IDs, which specify their package name in the Android ecosystem. Additionally, for forensic accuracy, each application version can be verified using its SHA-256 hash —a cryptographic representation that ensures the integrity of the application data. Among the list of applications installed on the victim’s phone, I identified one of particular interest—a trading application with the bundle ID com.ticno.olymptrade.

4) Based on the statement from the victim's family, they said that on September 20, 2023, he departed from his residence without informing anyone of his destination. So we need to know where the victim was located at that moment?
To identify the victim's location on September 20, 2023, we relied on the analysis of GPS-related data retrieved from the victim's Android device. GPS data plays a critical role in forensic investigations, as it can pinpoint a device's physical location based on its interactions with mapping applications, location services, or geotagged activities. In this case, the analysis focused on recent tasks and activity logs extracted using ALEAPP. The logs revealed that the victim had recently used the Google Maps application. The associated task data included key metadata, such as the Last_Time_Moved, which indicated that the application was active on September 20, 2023, at 23:50:29. This timestamp aligns with the family’s statement regarding the victim’s departure from his residence.

By correlating the timestamped activity logs with the visual evidence from the mapping application, we can reasonably conclude that the victim was at or near the Nile Ritz-Carlton in Cairo during the specified time. This data is vital for reconstructing the victim's movements and understanding the sequence of events leading up to the incident.
